# ICP Research Playbook

Where to find out what a buyer actually thinks. It's ordered roughly by how much signal each source gives per minute spent.

## 1. The user's own material (always first)

Sales call notes and transcripts, lost-deal emails, onboarding calls, support tickets, DMs and survey answers. Look for:

- The exact words a prospect used to describe their problem before they knew the product existed
- What they were using or doing before, and why it stopped working
- The moment they decided to look for help (the trigger event)
- Objections, word for word

## 2. Job postings for the buyer's role

This is underrated. A job description is a company writing down what this person is responsible for and measured on. Search the role title on job boards or with "[title] job description [industry]". Read three to five. Look for:

- Responsibilities that repeat across companies (the core of the job)
- Metrics and KPIs named in the posting (what they're judged on)
- Tools listed (what they already buy and live in)
- "Nice to haves" (where the role is heading)

## 3. Communities and forums

Reddit, industry Slack and Discord communities (only public ones), Hacker News, niche forums, Quora. Good search patterns:

- `site:reddit.com "[role or industry]" frustrated`
- `site:reddit.com "[role]" "anyone else"`
- `site:reddit.com "[problem your product solves]"`
- `"[role]" "biggest challenge" [year]`

Read the comments, not just the post. The top-voted replies tell you which pains other people in the role recognise.

## 4. Reviews of tools the buyer already pays for

Software review sites (G2, Capterra, TrustRadius and similar), app store reviews, and product-comparison threads. The "what do you dislike" sections are some of the most honest buyer language on the internet. Search for competitors and adjacent tools, not just direct rivals.

## 5. Podcasts, interviews and talks with people in the role

Search "[role] podcast", "[role] interview [year]", "[industry] conference [year] agenda". Show notes and transcripts reveal what people in the seat choose to talk about when given the mic. Conference agendas show what's top of mind this year, since organisers program what sells tickets.

## 6. Industry reports and surveys

"State of [industry] [year]", "[role] survey [year]". These are good for sizing a pain ("62% of ..."), but check who ran the survey. A vendor survey that conveniently proves the vendor's product is needed is weak evidence. Note the publisher next to any number you keep.

## 7. LinkedIn itself

Search results for `site:linkedin.com/posts "[role]" "[topic]"` sometimes surface posts by people in the role. Look at which posts in the niche get real comments from buyers (not just other creators). Treat this as supporting evidence, since search coverage of LinkedIn is patchy.

## 8. Newsletters and publications they read

Find where the buyer gets their news. It feeds the newsjacking agent later. Search "best newsletters for [role]" and "[industry] newsletter". Check which names come up repeatedly.

## Search discipline

- One idea per search. "CFO pain points" is too broad. "CFO month-end close takes too long reddit" is a search.
- Add the current year to searches where recency matters.
- Open the page before using anything from it. Record the date on the page.
- If a claim matters (a statistic, a trend), try to find it in a second independent source.
- Stop searching a thread when new sources stop telling you anything new. That's the sign you've found the real shape of it.

## What counts as a strong finding

- The same pain described independently in three or more places
- A verbatim quote that the user reads and says "that's exactly what my customers say"
- A trigger event (the moment someone starts looking for a solution) that you could build a post around

## What to be careful with

- Loud minorities. One angry thread isn't a market.
- Old material presented as current. Check dates.
- Vendor content dressed as research.
- Your own assumptions. If you catch yourself writing a pain nobody in the sources said, tag it `[My read]`.
